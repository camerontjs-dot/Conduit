# Runtime identity subject: requirements for independent oracle derivation

Decision: whether unchanged #128 preserves explicit native thread identity and
structured terminal provider failure through the offline native-client/Core and
durable reconstruction boundary. #123 qualification must finish first. This subject
does not include #123's AppModel/HTTP isolation implementation; a combined app/HTTP
journey is explicitly outside this candidate's claim.

## Identity and terminal authority

Native thread identity must come from an explicit, nonblank field in the correctly
correlated one-shot start/resume reply. Invoking resume with an intended identifier
is not an observation of the provider's returned thread. Missing, blank, unrelated,
malformed, stale or duplicate replies must not authorise delivery to an invented or
wrong thread. A held input must remain held when thread identity is unresolved.

A structured terminal rejection/error before assistant output should reconcile the
corresponding provider turn out of active and preserve the provider's safe structured
reason/code/type. Preserve provider-specific semantics rather than inventing one
normalised meaning from error prose. Quiet output, EOF, child exit or PTY capture-close
alone is not a structured terminal failure and must retain its weaker meaning.

Wrong-thread or wrong-turn replies/events cannot acquire authority over another turn.
A generic transport/RPC diagnostic must not become terminal provider truth without
the required identity and terminal evidence. A duplicate or stale start, output,
approval or completion must not resurrect an already terminal-failed turn. An explicit
new delivered input can begin a later turn without inheriting or rewriting the prior
failure. Task/runtime lifecycle, provider-turn status, objective acceptance and
verification remain separate facts with UNKNOWN preserved where appropriate.

## Durable consumer boundary

Preserve the safe failure receipt through durable logging and reconstruction after
runtime closure, without requiring the old provider process. Status/events projections
must agree on the affected terminal turn under the frozen subject's available Core
consumer boundary. Contradictory stale live flags cannot erase an authorised durable
failure for the current delivered input. Do not claim actual AppModel/HTTP endpoint
acceptance from a Core projection test.

Exercise the actual native client process boundary with an offline executable
provider fixture, not a cloud account or the operator provider installation. The
fixture should independently vary explicit/missing identity, correlation, ordering,
terminal-versus-transport errors, replay and later input. Derive expectations before
reading implementation or owner-generated fixtures. Freeze the native fixture and
oracle before the decisive controls; do not repair them to match the candidate.

## Evidence and non-claims

Record exact source, consumed files, fixture, command, process, input, durable artifact
and output identities, actual exits/waits and owned teardown. Challenge privacy using
synthetic sensitive-looking data only, and require durable/exported evidence to avoid
retaining that data. Do not publish private logs or operational credentials.

A wrong-thread delivery, invented identity, resurrection, lost terminal receipt or
unjustified status collapse is a product failure at the tested boundary. An invalid
native fixture, missing authority or unestablished isolated launch is an apparatus
block. Neither means that the task's objective was accepted or rejected. No real
provider performance, account behaviour, queue composition with other Drafts,
operator installation, current-main integration or release is established here.
